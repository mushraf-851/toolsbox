Ratio calculator — all modes explained

A ratio expresses the relative size of two or more values. This calculator handles all four key ratio operations: simplifying, solving proportions, scaling and comparing.

🔢 Simplifying ratios

Divide both parts by their GCD (Greatest Common Divisor). Example: 24:16 → GCD=8 → 3:2. The simplified form is the smallest integer ratio that represents the same proportion.

🔍 Finding missing values

Use cross-multiplication: if a:b = c:d and d is unknown, then d = (b×c)÷a. This is used in map scales, recipe adjustments and unit conversions.

📏 Scaling ratios

Multiply each part by a scale factor. To reach a specific total, multiply each part by (target total ÷ sum of parts). Used in mixing, recipes and financial allocations.

⚖️ Comparing ratios

Convert each ratio to a decimal (A÷B) and compare. Equivalent ratios have the same decimal value. Also checks if they share the same simplified form.

Frequently asked questions

How do you simplify a ratio step by step?
Step 1: Find the GCD of both numbers using the Euclidean algorithm. Step 2: Divide both parts by the GCD. Example: Simplify 36:24. GCD(36,24) = 12. 36÷12 = 3. 24÷12 = 2. Answer: 3:2. You can verify by checking that no number greater than 1 divides both 3 and 2 evenly.
How do you convert a ratio to percentage?
To convert ratio A:B to percentages: Total parts = A+B. A% = A/(A+B) × 100. B% = B/(A+B) × 100. Example: 3:2 → Total = 5. A = 3/5 × 100 = 60%. B = 2/5 × 100 = 40%.
What is the difference between ratio and rate?
A ratio compares two quantities of the same unit (e.g. 3 boys to 4 girls). A rate compares two quantities of different units (e.g. 60 km per hour). Both are expressed as fractions, but rates always carry units (km/h, $/kg, etc.).
How are ratios used in real life?
Ratios are everywhere: cooking recipes (2 cups flour : 1 cup sugar), maps (1:50,000 scale), finance (debt-to-equity ratio), photography (aspect ratio 16:9), pharmacy (medication dosages), and construction (concrete mix 1:2:3 cement:sand:aggregate).
