How EPF works in India

The Employee Provident Fund (EPF) is a mandatory retirement savings scheme for salaried employees in India. Both you and your employer contribute 12% of your basic salary each month. The fund earns tax-free interest declared by EPFO every year.

👤 Employee contribution

You contribute 12% of (Basic + DA) every month. This entire 12% goes into your EPF account. On a ₹25,000 basic salary, you contribute ₹3,000 per month = ₹36,000 per year.

🏢 Employer contribution split

Employer also contributes 12% of basic, but it splits: 3.67% goes to EPF (your corpus) and 8.33% goes to EPS (Employee Pension Scheme) which gives you a monthly pension after retirement.

💰 Interest calculation

Interest at 8.25% is calculated monthly on the running balance and credited annually at year end. Compounding makes this the best risk-free return in India — better than FD and PPF for salaried employees.

📋 Tax benefits (EEE)

EPF is one of few "EEE" investments — Exempt at contribution (80C), Exempt on interest earned, Exempt on maturity. All three stages tax-free, making the effective return much higher than the stated 8.25%.

Frequently asked questions

How is EPF interest calculated monthly?
Interest is calculated on the running balance each month: Monthly interest = Opening balance × (Annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100). However, it is only credited to your account at the end of the financial year (March 31). The interest itself is not compounded monthly — it compounds annually.
Can I increase my EPF contribution beyond 12%?
Yes! You can opt for Voluntary Provident Fund (VPF) — you can contribute up to 100% of your basic salary. VPF earns the same interest rate as EPF and enjoys all the same tax benefits. You cannot increase your employer's contribution beyond 12% though.
What happens to EPF when I change jobs?
You should transfer your EPF balance to your new employer using the EPFO portal (member.epfindia.gov.in). The UAN (Universal Account Number) stays the same across jobs. If you withdraw instead of transferring, you lose tax benefits if you haven't completed 5 years of continuous service.
What is the EPS pension I will get at retirement?
EPS pension = (Pensionable salary × Pensionable service) ÷ 70. Pensionable salary is capped at ₹15,000/month. Maximum pension = ₹15,000 × 35 years ÷ 70 = ₹7,500/month. If your salary exceeds ₹15,000, you can opt for higher pension under the amended EPS scheme.
