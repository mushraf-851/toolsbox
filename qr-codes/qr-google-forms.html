📖 Complete Guide: How to Boost Survey Responses with QR Codes

Google Forms is one of the most widely used tools for surveys, quizzes, feedback collection, and registrations — but its long, complex URLs are a real barrier to participation when shared in print or in person. A QR code solves this instantly: anyone with a smartphone camera can open your form in under two seconds, with zero typing and zero typos.

Standard Link vs. Pre-Filled Link

For most use cases, the standard form share link (or its shortened forms.gle version) is all you need — anyone who scans it lands on a blank form ready to fill out. Pre-filled links are useful when you want to track the source of responses or pre-set certain answers. For example, an event organiser running multiple sessions could create a separate pre-filled QR code for each session, with the "Session" field already filled in, making it easy to filter responses by session afterward.

Getting Your Google Forms Link

Open your form in edit mode, click the "Send" button in the top right corner, then select the link icon (chain-link symbol) in the send dialog. Check "Shorten URL" to get a clean forms.gle link, which produces a simpler, more reliable QR code than the full docs.google.com URL. Click "Copy" and paste it into this generator.

To create a pre-filled link, open the ⋮ (three-dot) menu while viewing your form (not editing it), select "Get pre-filled link," fill in the values you want pre-set, click "Get link" at the bottom, then copy that URL instead.

Pro Tips for Maximising Response Rates

  • Check sharing permissions first: Make sure your form's access is set to "Anyone with the link can respond" — otherwise scanners will hit a permission error.
  • Keep forms short: Response rates drop sharply after 5-7 questions. A QR code gets people to the form, but brevity keeps them completing it.
  • Add a clear call to action near the code: "Scan to share your feedback" performs far better than a bare QR code with no context.
  • Use SVG for printing: Always download SVG for posters, banners, and any large-format printed material — it scales without quality loss.
  • Test on multiple devices: Confirm the QR code opens correctly on both iOS and Android before distributing it widely.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I create a QR code for a Google Form?+
Open your Google Form, click Send, click the link icon, copy the URL (or use the short forms.gle link), paste it into the generator, click Generate, then download as PNG or SVG.
Where do I find the share link for my Google Form?+
Open your form in Google Forms, click the "Send" button in the top right, select the link icon tab, optionally check "Shorten URL" for a forms.gle link, then click Copy.
Can I pre-fill answers in my Google Form QR code?+
Yes. In Google Forms, click the three-dot menu and select "Get pre-filled link", fill in the answers you want pre-set, click "Get link", then paste that pre-filled URL into the generator instead of the standard form link.
Does this Google Forms QR code expire?+
No. This is a static QR code — the form link is encoded directly in the image. It works forever with no subscription, server, or expiry date, as long as the form itself remains open to responses.
Will the QR code still work if I edit my form later?+
Yes. The form URL stays the same even after you add, remove, or edit questions. The QR code will always open the current live version of your form.
Where should I display my Google Forms QR code?+
Best locations: classroom whiteboards, event check-in tables, printed flyers, email signatures, presentation slides, table tents at conferences, and posters in common areas. Anywhere people have a spare moment with their phone is a good spot.
Is my form link stored on your servers?+
No. All QR code generation happens entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your form link never leaves your device — never sent to any server, never stored, never tracked.
