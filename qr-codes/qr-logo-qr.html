📖 Complete Guide to Logo QR Codes

A logo QR code is a standard QR code with your brand's logo, icon, or image embedded in the centre. It encodes the same data as any QR code — a URL, text, contact card, or payment link — but with visual branding that makes it instantly recognisable and significantly more likely to be scanned by your target audience.

How Logo Overlay Works Without Breaking the QR Code

QR codes have built-in error correction — the ability to recover data even when part of the code is obscured or damaged. Level H error correction (which this tool sets automatically when you upload a logo) can tolerate up to 30% of the QR code being obscured. As long as your logo covers less than 25% of the QR area, the code remains fully scannable. Our generator automatically sizes the logo to stay within this safe zone.

Choosing the Right Logo Format

For the cleanest result, use a PNG with a transparent background. This allows the white backing behind the logo to blend seamlessly with the QR code background. A JPG logo will work but will have a white square backing that may not match if you're using a non-white QR background. SVG logos at any resolution will always render crisp and clean.

Dot Styles & Eye Styles — What They Do

Dot styles change the shape of the individual QR modules. Squares (default) are the most scan-reliable. Circles create a softer, modern look. Diamonds add a premium, geometric feel. Rounded dots combine the reliability of squares with a softer edge.

Eye styles change the three corner finder patterns — the distinctive boxes that help scanners orient the code. Standard is the most reliable. Rounded creates a friendly, modern look. Circle eyes are clean and minimal. Diamond eyes are bold and distinctive.

Pro Tips for Logo QR Codes

  • Square logos work best — they fit neatly in the QR centre without distortion. If your logo is wide/landscape, the circle frame can help crop it attractively.
  • Use the rounded frame for tech and app brands — it looks modern and aligns with app icon aesthetics that audiences associate with digital brands.
  • Gradient adds visual flair — but keep both colours dark enough to maintain contrast against the background. Dark purple to dark blue works well.
  • Increase QR size for complex logos — at 300×300px, very detailed logos may appear blurry. Use 400-600px for complex logos and download at that size for print.
  • Test scan rate before bulk printing — print a test at 50%, 75%, and 100% scale and scan each one. If any fail, reduce logo size or increase QR size.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I add a logo to a QR code?+
Enter your URL or content, then click the upload area to add your logo. The logo is automatically centred in the QR code and sized to stay within the 25% safe zone. We auto-set error correction to Level H to keep the code scannable.
Will the logo make the QR code unscannable?+
Not with our generator. QR codes have error correction that tolerates up to 30% obstruction at Level H. We auto-set Level H when you add a logo and constrain the logo to under 25% of the QR area — so it remains fully scannable.
What's the best logo format to upload?+
PNG with a transparent background gives the cleanest result because the white frame blends with any background colour. JPG works but always has a white square. SVG is also supported and always renders crisp at any size.
What are dot styles and eye styles?+
Dot styles change the shape of individual QR modules — square (default), circle, diamond, or rounded. Eye styles change the three corner finder patterns — standard, rounded, circle, or diamond. They dramatically change the QR's look while keeping it fully functional.
Can I use gradient colours with a logo QR?+
Yes. Toggle the Gradient switch and choose two colours. The QR transitions from the start colour to the end colour. For best scanning, keep both colours dark and maintain high contrast against the background. Dark purple to dark blue works especially well.
Is my logo stored on your servers?+
No. All processing — QR generation and logo overlay — happens entirely in your browser using JavaScript. Your logo and URL never leave your device.
Can I change the URL without reprinting?+
With a static QR code, the URL is encoded in the image — changing it requires reprinting. If you need flexibility, use a URL shortener (like Bitly) as the QR destination, then update the redirect target whenever needed. The same QR code always points to the new page.
