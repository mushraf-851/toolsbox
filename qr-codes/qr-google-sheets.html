📖 Complete Guide to Google Sheets QR Codes

Google Sheets is one of the most widely used collaboration tools in the world, but sharing spreadsheet links remains cumbersome — long URLs, copy-paste errors, and outdated attachments are constant pain points. A Google Sheets QR code solves all of this. One scan opens the live, always-current spreadsheet on any device, no typing required.

Choosing the Right Sharing Mode

This generator offers three modes. Direct Link is the standard approach — paste your share link and the QR encodes it directly. Use the access level selector to ensure viewers get read-only or edit access as intended. Template mode converts your URL to a "Make a copy" link. This is ideal for workshops, classrooms, and conferences where each person needs their own version of a budget tracker, project plan, or invoice template. Published mode is for sheets published publicly via File → Share → Publish to web, which opens without requiring a Google account.

Finding Your Sheet Tab GID

When you click a specific tab in Google Sheets, the URL updates to include #gid=XXXXXXXX at the end. Copy this number and paste it into the Sheet Tab GID field to create a QR that opens directly to that specific sheet, bypassing the default first tab.

Pro Tips for Teams

  • Laminated QR cards: Print and laminate QR codes pointing to shared trackers. Stick them on whiteboards or desks for permanent access without any link sharing friction.
  • Pair with View-only access: For dashboards and reports, use View-only QR codes. This prevents accidental edits while keeping data accessible.
  • Template QRs at workshops: Print template QR codes on handouts. Attendees scan to get their own copy — no email collection needed.
  • Update the sheet, not the QR: Because the QR encodes a live link, you can update the spreadsheet content as often as needed. The same QR code always opens the latest version.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I create a QR code for a Google Sheet?+
Open your Google Sheet, click Share (top right), set "Anyone with the link" to the appropriate access level, copy the link, paste it here, and click Generate. Your QR code is ready to download in seconds.
What's the difference between Direct Link and Template mode?+
Direct Link opens the shared spreadsheet — viewers see the same file, and access is controlled by your sharing settings. Template mode converts the URL to a "Make a copy" link so each person who scans gets their own independent private copy of the sheet.
Can I link to a specific tab in my Google Sheet?+
Yes. When you click a specific tab in Google Sheets, the URL changes to include #gid=XXXXXXXX. Copy the number after #gid= and enter it in the Sheet Tab GID field. The QR will open directly to that tab.
Does the QR code update automatically when the spreadsheet changes?+
Yes. The QR encodes a link to the live spreadsheet. Any updates you make to the data in Google Sheets are immediately visible to anyone who scans the QR — no need to regenerate the QR code.
Does the QR code expire?+
No. The QR image never expires. The link may stop working if you delete the spreadsheet or revoke sharing permissions, but the QR code itself works forever.
Is my Google Sheets URL stored on your servers?+
No. All QR code generation happens entirely in your browser. Your spreadsheet URL never leaves your device and is never stored on our servers.
Can I add my company logo to the QR code?+
Yes. Expand the Customise section, upload your logo (PNG, JPG, or SVG up to 500 KB), and it will be placed in the centre of the QR. We automatically switch to Level H error correction to keep the code scannable with the logo overlay.
